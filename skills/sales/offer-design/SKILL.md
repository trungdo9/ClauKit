# Offer Design — Offer, Magnet, Channel, Amplifier

> Many sales problems are offer problems in disguise. Fix the offer before buying traffic for it.

## When this skill activates

**Implicit:** "nobody's converting", "our offer is weak", "should we lower the price", "what lead magnet should we build", "we need more leads", "should we start paid ads", "how do we get referrals / partners to send leads".
**Explicit:** "Use the offer-design skill to [task]."
**Routed from:** `/mk:sales offer`; consulted by [[outbound]] (what the outreach invites people to).

## Scope

Covers:
- Offer construction and audit via the value equation.
- Guarantees, proof stacks, risk reversal, bonus stacks, payment structure.
- Lead magnets: type, format, standalone value, capture, and the nurture that must exist first.
- The four lead-generation channels, the sequencing rule, and activity commitment.

- Price-point research and packaging → [[product-marketing]], `paywalls`.

## Sequence: offer → magnet → nurture → channel → amplifier

In that order. Ads before a validated offer, a magnet before its nurture exists, affiliates before the offer obviously converts — each is a common way to burn budget.

## 1. The value equation

```
              Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood of achievement
Value  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                   Time delay × Effort & sacrifice
```

Every design choice raises the numerator or lowers the denominator. That is the whole job.

| Lever | Raise / lower it by |
|---|---|
| **Dream outcome** ↑ | The transformation in the buyer's own words (from [[customer-research]]), not the deliverable they nominally pay for |
| **Perceived likelihood** ↑ | Real proof (case studies with measured outcomes), guarantees, risk reversal, specificity |
| **Time delay** ↓ | Compress time to first visible result; done-for-you > done-with-you > DIY |
| **Effort & sacrifice** ↓ | Remove steps, decisions, and habits the buyer must take on |

**Price is rarely the lever.** Rebuild the equation before discounting. Touch price only when the other levers are exhausted.

### Offer audit

Score each lever 1–10 *as the buyer sees it* (from interviews, sales-call notes, lost-deal reasons — cite which). The weakest lever is where the next work goes.

### Guarantees — a core element, not an afterthought

| Type | Shape | Fits when |
|---|---|---|
| Unconditional | Money back, no questions | Unit economics can absorb refunds; trust is the main objection |
| Conditional | Outcome-based, buyer does defined work | Results depend on buyer effort |
| Anti-guarantee | Explicit no-refund, with a stated reason | Exclusive/high-touch deliverables consumed immediately |
| Implied | Deliver before payment, or pay-on-result | You can carry the risk and want to remove it entirely |

Every guarantee must be honorable — written terms, refund exposure modeled. Check consumer-protection rules in the selling jurisdiction; ask which apply.

### Offer stack (advanced)

- Core offer + bonuses, each bonus resolving one specific objection.
- Scarcity and urgency only when real (capacity, cohort dates). Manufactured scarcity is a trust and often a legal problem.
- Payment structure matched to the buyer's cash flow: upfront, split, outcome-based, subscription.

## 2. Lead magnets

A complete solution to a **narrow** problem, given in exchange for contact details. It must be valuable standalone: a buyer who stops there should still feel well served.

| Type | Does | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Solve** | A usable result now — calculator, plan, diagnostic, template | You sell a how-to and want to prove mastery with a small win |
| **Educate** | Reframes the problem as bigger than they thought | High-ticket; buyer underestimates cost of inaction |
| **Sample** | A literal piece of the paid product — chapter, session, trial | Experience products where tasting builds belief fastest |

Rules:
- **The magnet picks the buyer.** Its sophistication sets who opts in — match it to the target.
- **One magnet per persona per stage.** One magnet for three buyer types is generic to all of them.
- **Designed to be finished.** Unconsumed magnets rarely convert; measure consumption, not just opt-ins.
- **Never build capture you can't honor.** Welcome sequence, nurture, and next-step offer exist before launch.

## 3. The four lead-generation channels

| Channel | Audience | Cost profile | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm outreach | People who know you | Free, effortful, doesn't scale | First customers; offer validation |
| Free content | Strangers becoming an audience | Free, effortful, compounds | Durable attention and authority |
| Cold outreach | Strangers | Cheap, scales with systems → [[outbound]] | B2B, niche, direct sales motion |
| Paid ads | Rented attention | Cash, dial-up-able → `/mk:ads` | Proven offers with known unit economics |

**Sequencing rule:** warm outreach to validate → one of content or cold to build a repeatable engine → paid only once evidence shows the offer converts at a CAC the customer lifetime value can pay for.

**One channel before two.** Dominate one before adding another, and commit to a high, sustained daily activity volume for a fixed period before judging it — a frequent failure is insufficient volume, not a bad plan. The volume target is set from team capacity and stated up front, along with the kill criteria.

Iterate **more → better → new**: push volume, then improve conversion, and only add a new channel once the current one plateaus.

## 4. Reach amplifiers

Activate in this order, each only after the previous condition holds:

1. **Customers (referrals)** — ask at the moment of delivered value; effortless mechanic; reward both sides. Mechanics → `/mk:growth referral`.
2. **Employees** — trained and rewarded to post and introduce.
3. **Affiliates and partners** — formal affiliates, bundled offers with strategic partners, creators with overlapping audiences. Only after the offer visibly converts; affiliates will not rescue a weak offer. Commission structures are modeled against unit economics, not copied from a norm.
4. **Agencies** — never for a channel you have not proven yourself.

## 5. Unit-economics gate

Before scaling any channel, compute from the user's own data:
- CAC per channel (fully loaded), LTV, LTV:CAC, CAC payback period.
- Kill criteria set **before** launch, not after the money is spent.

Thresholds for "healthy" are the business's to set (gross margin and cash position drive them). Common rules of thumb exist; if quoted, label them as heuristics, not targets. The finance side of this → `/mk:finance model`.

## Guardrails

- **No invented conversion figures or lift claims** (`.claude/workflows/marketing-rules.md` §2). Opt-in ranges, "guarantees double conversion", target value-to-price ratios, and commission norms are not stated as fact.
- **No fabricated proof.** Case studies, testimonials, and outcomes in an offer must be real and permissioned; otherwise `[NEEDS DATA]`. A fake proof stack destroys the likelihood lever it was meant to raise.
- **Honest scarcity and guarantees only**, within the selling jurisdiction's consumer rules.

## Before proceeding

1. What is sold, to whom, at what price, and what result does the buyer actually want?
2. What proof exists today (measured outcomes, permissioned references)?
3. Current conversion data at each funnel step, if any — or is this pre-launch?
4. Which channels does the team already run, and what CAC / LTV data exists?
