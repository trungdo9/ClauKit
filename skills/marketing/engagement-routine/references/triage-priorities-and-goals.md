# Triage, priorities, and goals

How to spend limited engagement time well — what to do first, who to focus on, and how to know it is
working without an analytics surface.

## Triage: the priority order

When time is short, do the highest-return things first:

1. **Golden-hour comments on the fresh post** — the first ~15–20 minutes. The biggest reach lever there is.
2. **DMs** — relationships and conversions live here. Reply while warm.
3. **Negative or urgent** — complaints, mistakes, anything time-sensitive. Acknowledge fast and take
   specifics private. A genuine crisis needs a named escalation path, not a comment reply.
4. **Older comments** still worth a thoughtful reply.
5. **Proactive comments** — real value on ICP, peer, and leader posts. This borrows their audience.
6. **Skip:** spam and trolls. Delete or ignore. Do not feed them.

### Worked example — "I have 15 minutes"

Reply to every comment on the post that went out an hour ago (golden-hour leftovers) → answer the 2 new
DMs → one genuine reply to a complaint, with "let's sort this out in DMs" → if time remains, one
value-adding comment on a niche leader's post. Stop. **Done beats perfect.**

## The right people, not vanity volume

Engagement should reach **the people who matter to the business** — the ICP and niche peers — not maximize
raw volume. A flood of comments from the wrong crowd, or from bait, builds nothing. Make every interaction
count; engage where it compounds.

## Map to a goal (and signals that can actually be seen)

Pick **one goal** for engagement — relationships, leads, community, or support — and **2–3 meaningful
signals**, not vanity metrics:

- **Comment depth** — real conversations, not one-emoji replies.
- **DM conversations started**, and where they lead.
- **Replies to your replies** — people coming back.
- **Proactive comments that sparked a reply** — the audience-borrowing actually working.

**Honesty about measurement:** ClauKit has **no social analytics surface**, so these are read **natively** —
the platform's own insights, the inbox, and direct observation. **Never fabricate performance data and
never promise a specific number.** Engagement is a leading indicator; judge it by depth and direction over
weeks, not by a vanity count today.

**Snapshot the numbers weekly.** Meta retains roughly 90 days of insight, and a baseline taken after the
work started is not a baseline. Take it before the first post. Reporting and attribution → [[analytics]].

## What to stop doing

- Chasing likes and followers as the scoreboard — the weakest signals available.
- Replying to everything everywhere, thinly, instead of the right things well.
- Engagement bait ("comment YES") and engagement pods — detected and penalized.
- Buying or automating engagement — fake, detected, and self-defeating.
