## Headline formulas

Each formula is a shape, not a script. Fill it with the ICP's own words from `plans/marketing-context.md`. The examples below are real-product illustrations of the shape — never ship an example as-is.

### Outcome-focused

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| `{Achieve outcome} without {pain point}` | Understand how users really experience your site without drowning in numbers |
| `{Achieve outcome} by {how the product makes it possible}` | Generate more leads by seeing which companies visit your site |
| `Turn {input} into {outcome}` | Turn your hard-earned sales into repeat customers |
| `{Achieve outcome} in {timeframe}` | Get your tax refund in 10 days |

### Problem-focused

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| `Never {unpleasant event} again` | Never miss a sales opportunity again |
| `{Question naming the main pain}` | Hate returning stuff to Amazon? |
| `Stop {pain}. Start {pleasure}.` | Stop chasing invoices. Start getting paid on time. |

### Audience-focused

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| `{Category} for {audience}` | Advanced analytics for Shopify e-commerce |
| `{Category} for {audience} to {job to be done}` | An online whiteboard for teams to ideate and brainstorm together |
| `You don't have to {skill/resource} to {outcome}` | You don't have to be an SEO pro to rank higher |

### Differentiation-focused

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| `The {opposite of the usual process} way to {outcome}` | The easiest way to turn your passion into income |
| `The {category} that {key differentiator}` | The CRM that updates itself |

### Proof-focused

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| `{Number} {people} use {product} to {outcome}` | 50,000 marketers use it to send better emails |
| `{Single key benefit, stated flat}` | Sound clear in online meetings |

Proof-focused headlines need a real number. No number in `marketing-context.md` and none supplied by the user → pick a different shape or mark the slot `[NEEDS DATA]`. Never estimate a customer count.

### Additional shapes

- `The simple way to {outcome}` — The simple way to track your time
- `Finally, {category} that {benefit}` — Finally, accounting software that doesn't fight you
- `{Outcome} without {common pain}` — Build your website without writing code
- `Get {benefit} from your {existing thing}` — Get more revenue from your existing traffic
- `{Verb} your {thing} like {admirable example}` — Market your SaaS like a Fortune 500
- `What if you could {outcome}?` — What if you could close deals faster?
- `Everything you need to {outcome}` — Everything you need to launch your course
- `The {adjective} {category} built for {audience}` — The lightweight CRM built for startups

## Landing page section types

### Core sections

**Hero (above the fold)** — headline, subheadline, primary CTA, supporting visual, optional social-proof bar.

**Social proof bar** — customer logos (recognizable beats numerous), one key metric, star rating with review count, or a short testimonial snippet.

**Problem / pain** — articulate their problem better than they can. Aim for recognition ("that's exactly my situation"). Hint at the cost of not solving it.

**Solution / benefits** — bridge problem to product. 3-5 benefits, not 10. Each one: headline (the outcome) + explanation (1-2 sentences) + proof if it exists.

**How it works** — 3-4 numbered steps. Reduces perceived complexity. Each step: action + what they get.

**Final CTA** — recap the value prop, repeat the primary CTA, add risk reversal (guarantee, free trial, cancel anytime).

### Supporting sections

| Section | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Testimonials | Credibility | Full quotes, name + role + company, specific results over vague praise |
| Case studies | Proof of outcome | Problem → solution → results, with real metrics only |
| Use cases | Self-identification | "For {role} who need {X}" |
| Personas / "built for" | Answers "is this for me?" | Explicit role callouts |
| FAQ | Objection handling | 5-10 real objections, not softballs |
| Comparison | vs. competitors or vs. status quo (spreadsheets, manual work) | Table or side-by-side |
| Integrations | "Works with your stack" | Logos of tools you actually connect to |
| Founder story | Emotional differentiation | Why you built it, what you believe |
| Demo / product tour | Shows, doesn't tell | Video, GIF, interactive |
| Pricing preview | Moves decision-makers | "From $X/mo" even on non-pricing pages |
| Guarantee / risk reversal | Removes friction | Money-back, free trial terms |
| Stats | Credibility at a glance | Real numbers only |

## Page structure templates

**Feature-heavy (weak)**

```
Hero → Feature 1 → Feature 2 → Feature 3 → Feature 4 → CTA
```

A list, not an argument. Nothing here handles an objection.

**Varied and engaging (strong)**

```
1. Hero with clear value prop
2. Social proof bar
3. Problem / pain
4. How it works (3 steps)
5. Key benefits (2-3)
6. Testimonial
7. Use cases or personas
8. Comparison to alternatives
9. Case study snippet
10. FAQ
11. Final CTA with guarantee
```

**Compact landing page** (ad traffic, brevity matters)

```
Hero → social proof bar → 3 benefits → testimonial → how it works (3 steps) → final CTA with guarantee
```

**Enterprise / B2B**

```
Hero (outcome headline) → logo bar → business-pain problem section → solution overview →
use cases by role → security/compliance → integrations → case study with metrics →
ROI/value → demo or contact CTA
```

**Product launch**

```
Hero with launch announcement → video demo → 3-5 feature highlights → before/after →
early testimonials → launch pricing or early access → CTA with (genuine) urgency
```

## Section writing tips

### Problem section

Openers that create recognition:

- "You know the feeling…"
- "If you're like most {role}…"
- "Every day, {audience} struggles with…"

Then name the specific frustration, the time or money it costs, and the knock-on effect on their work.

### Benefits section

Per benefit: **headline** = the outcome they get · **body** = how it works in 1-2 sentences · **proof** = a number, a testimonial, or an example. Skip the proof line rather than invent one.

### How it works

Each step: numbered (creates progress), simple verb ("Connect", "Set up", "Get"), outcome-oriented.

```
1. Connect your tools (takes 2 minutes)
2. Set your preferences
3. Get automated reports every Monday
```

### Testimonial selection

Keep quotes that carry a specific result ("cut reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes"), before/after context, and a role + company. Cut quotes that only say "Great product!", "Love it!", "Easy to use!" — they consume space and prove nothing.

Testimonials are quoted, never authored. If the user has no real quotes, leave the slot as `[NEEDS TESTIMONIAL]` in the draft.

## Clarity and message-market fit

Headline formulas give you the shape. These three tests tell you whether the line actually works — whether it is clear, whether it maps to how the reader already thinks, and whether it lands with the right person.

### The "Now you can" test

Prefix any headline or benefit line with **"Now you can…"**. If the result is both **compelling** and **true**, the line is doing its job. If it reads vague, obvious, or as a stretch, rewrite it. The test works because the prefix forces the copy to name a concrete new ability the reader did not have before — feature-speak and buzzwords collapse under it.

| Line | "Now you can…" | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Powerful analytics platform" | …have a powerful analytics platform | Fails — a description, not a new ability |
| "See which companies visit your site" | …see which companies visit your site | Works — concrete and true |
| "Streamline your workflow" | …streamline your workflow | Fails — vague, unfalsifiable |
| "Send unlimited docs, images, and audio in one place" | …send unlimited docs, images, and audio in one place | Works |

Use it as a filter, not a generator: draft with the formulas, then run every candidate through it and keep the survivors.

### The Human Action Model (hero narrative spine)

From Ludwig von Mises: a person acts only when three things line up. Heroes that convert follow the same three beats.

1. **Current discomfort** — the felt problem, in the reader's own words. They must recognize themselves.
2. **Better vision** — the more satisfying state, clearly imagined.
3. **Path to action** — the belief that *this specific step* closes the gap. The product is the bridge; the CTA is how they cross it.

Miss a beat and the reader stalls: no discomfort = no reason to move, no vision = no destination, no path = no reason to believe you are the way there.

| Beat | Usual home | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| Current discomfort | Eyebrow, subhead, or problem-framed headline | "You shouldn't have to feel awkward sending out your scheduling link" |
| Better vision | Headline or subhead | "Scheduling that feels considerate, not one-sided" |
| Path to action | CTA + supporting proof | "Start scheduling free" |

The hero states the transformation; the rest of the page substantiates each beat.

### The Perception Gap

The same benefit reads as a selling point to one segment and a red flag to another. The gap sits between what you think you are saying and what a given reader hears through their own risk tolerance.

| Benefit as written | Early adopter hears | Risk-averse enterprise hears |
|---|---|---|
| "Move fast — ship in a weekend" | Speed, momentum | Immature, unstable |
| "Brand-new approach" | Innovative edge | Unproven |
| "Enterprise-grade security and SLAs" | Bloated, slow, expensive | Safe, trustworthy |
| "Trusted by the Fortune 500" | Not built for me | Proven, de-risked |

The fix is not softer copy — it is matching the framing to the reader's risk tolerance. Same product, two audiences:

- *Startup page:* "Ship your first integration this afternoon. No sales calls, no procurement."
- *Enterprise page:* "SOC 2 Type II, uptime SLA, and a named implementation lead."

When one page must serve both, do not average them into mush. Segment (separate pages, or a persona split) and let each read its own version of the truth.

### Clarity beats cleverness

The mechanism: clear copy lets the right buyer self-qualify fast and the wrong one bounce early, so every downstream metric improves. Clever copy that needs decoding adds a comprehension tax at the moment attention is scarcest.

Practical rule: if a reader has to pause to work out what you mean, you already lost them. Forced to choose between a clever line and a clear one, ship the clear one — then use the three tests above to make the clear line compelling.
