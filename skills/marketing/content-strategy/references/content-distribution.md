# Content Distribution Spine

The "distribute" half of content strategy. Creating a great piece is table stakes; the leverage is in getting it seen. Expands the **Distribution and repurposing** section of `SKILL.md`.

Boundary: the ORB channel-type playbook lives in [[launch]]; atomization and repurposing execution (podcast → clips, blog → thread) lives in [[social-content]]. This file consolidates the strategy that ties them together — do not re-derive ORB elsewhere.

## Create Once, Distribute Twice

One exceptional piece, reformatted across channels — not a fresh piece per platform. The math: a flagship plus ten repurposed cuts reaches far more people than eleven mediocre native posts, at a fraction of the effort.

The discipline is **designing the piece to be distributed**:
- Write subheads that read as standalone social posts.
- Structure sections modularly so they can be lifted out and stand alone.
- Pre-identify the pull quotes, stats, and frames that become graphics or clips.
- Know the atomized outputs before writing, so the source piece contains them.

Treat the flagship as the master; every channel gets a cut derived from it.

## The ORB framework as a funnel

Own / Rent / Borrow, read as discovery → engagement → conversion:

| Layer | Channels | Funnel role | You control |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Borrowed** | Podcasts, guest posts, partnerships, PR, other people's audiences | Discovery / breakthrough | Nothing — it is a loan |
| **Rented** | Social platforms, ad networks, marketplaces | Engagement / reach | The content, not the audience or algorithm |
| **Owned** | Email list, blog, community, product | Conversion / retention | Everything — the durable asset |

The strategic move: use borrowed and rented reach to funnel strangers into owned channels where you can convert and retain them. Borrowed and rented are rented land; owned is the only asset you keep.

## The ownership paradox

Most teams invert the priority — the bulk of effort goes to borrowed and rented channels they do not control, while the owned assets that actually convert are neglected. The paradox is that the channels getting least attention (email, blog, community) are the ones that compound and cannot be revoked. Rebalance so owned is the destination for all upstream effort.

## Failure modes

- **Spray-and-pray** — publishing across every platform with no flagship and no repurposing system. Effort scatters, nothing compounds, each post starts from zero.
- **Platform dependency** — building the audience on rented land. Facebook's organic reach collapse (roughly 20% down to low single digits as the platform monetized) is the canonical case; any rented channel can throttle, deprioritize, or de-platform with no recourse. The lesson is not "avoid rented" — it is "never let rented be the endpoint."

## Platform half-lives

Content decays at wildly different rates. Match the piece to the channel's shelf life:

| Channel | Rough half-life | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Twitter/X post | Minutes–hours | Post often; repost; thread for reach |
| Instagram / Facebook | ~a day | Frequent cadence; stories ephemeral by design |
| LinkedIn post | ~a day, longer for strong performers | Fewer, higher-effort posts |
| TikTok / Reels / Shorts | Days–weeks (algorithmic resurfacing) | Evergreen hooks can resurface long after posting |
| YouTube video | Months–years | Search-driven; compounds like a blog post |
| Blog post / SEO | Years | The long tail; the compounding asset |
| Email | Sent once, archived and repurposable | One-shot attention; harvest into other formats |

Short half-life channels reward frequency and repetition; long half-life channels reward depth and evergreen framing. Owned, long-half-life formats are where distribution effort compounds.

## The content distribution flywheel

Distribution is a loop that feeds itself, not a linear checklist:

1. **Create** one exceptional flagship (guide, video, podcast, original research) with distribution hooks built in.
2. **Atomize** it into channel-native cuts — clips, threads, carousels, quote graphics, email, subhead posts.
3. **Distribute** across owned → rented → borrowed, routing everything back to owned.
4. **Engage** with responses; capture the questions, objections, and reactions.
5. **Feed back** — engagement surfaces the next flagship topic, and top-performing atoms signal what to make more of.

Each turn lowers the cost of the next piece and grows the owned audience that amplifies it. This is why consistent distributors pull away from one-off publishers over time.

## Atomization checklist (per flagship)

For each major piece, produce (platform-native execution → [[social-content]]):

- [ ] 3–5 standalone social posts from the subheads and key points
- [ ] 1 thread (X) or carousel (LinkedIn / Instagram) of the core argument
- [ ] 2–4 short-form video clips, if the source is video or podcast
- [ ] 1–2 quote or stat graphics
- [ ] 1 email to the owned list linking the flagship
- [ ] A repost / reshare schedule spanning the piece's half-life — never post once and move on

Record these cuts in the calendar's `Repurpose cuts` column at planning time, not after publication.
