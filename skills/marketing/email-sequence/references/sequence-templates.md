# Sequence Templates

Email-by-email blueprints for the four load-bearing lifecycle sequences. Each is a starting cadence, not a fixed prescription — adjust length to sales-cycle length, product complexity, and relationship stage. Fill subjects and bodies against `plans/marketing-context.md` (ICP, positioning, brand voice, forbidden words).

Contents:
- Welcome sequence (post-signup)
- Lead nurture sequence (pre-sale)
- Re-engagement sequence
- Onboarding sequence (product users)

---

## Welcome sequence (post-signup)

**Trigger:** signup / list opt-in. **Length:** 5–7 emails over 12–14 days. **Goal:** activate, build trust, convert.
**Exit:** converted to the sequence goal, or enrolled in a higher-priority sequence (onboarding, dunning).

**Email 1 — Welcome (immediate)**
- Subject shape: "Welcome to [Product] — here's your first step"
- Deliver what was promised (lead magnet, access, credentials)
- One next action
- Set expectations: what you'll send, how often

**Email 2 — Quick win (day 1–2)**
- Subject shape: "Get your first [result] in 10 minutes"
- Enable a small, fast success; build confidence
- Link to one helpful resource

**Email 3 — Story / why (day 3–4)**
- Subject shape: "Why we built [Product]"
- Origin story or mission; connect emotionally
- Show you understand the problem they arrived with

**Email 4 — Social proof (day 5–6)**
- Subject shape: "How [Customer] achieved [Result]"
- Case study or testimonial that matches their segment
- Soft CTA to explore

**Email 5 — Overcome objection (day 7–8)**
- Subject shape: "'I don't have time for X' — sound familiar?"
- Name the common hesitation, reframe the obstacle
- Show the easy path forward

**Email 6 — Core feature (day 9–11)**
- Subject shape: "Have you tried [Feature] yet?"
- Highlight an underused capability with a clear benefit
- Direct CTA to try it

**Email 7 — Conversion (day 12–14)**
- Subject shape: "Ready to [upgrade / buy / commit]?"
- Summarize value, make the offer explicit
- Urgency only if genuine; add risk reversal (guarantee, trial)

## Lead nurture sequence (pre-sale)

**Trigger:** lead-magnet download or high-intent content opt-in. **Length:** 6–8 emails over 2–3 weeks.
**Goal:** trust -> preference -> offer. **Exit:** demo booked, purchased, or moved to a sales-owned sequence.

**Email 1 — Deliver + introduce (immediate)** — hand over the lead magnet, brief intro to who you are, preview what's coming.
**Email 2 — Expand on the topic (day 2–3)** — an insight adjacent to the magnet; establishes expertise; light CTA to content.
**Email 3 — Problem deep-dive (day 4–5)** — articulate their problem better than they can; hint at the solution shape.
**Email 4 — Solution framework (day 6–8)** — your approach/methodology; educational, not salesy; builds toward the product.
**Email 5 — Case study (day 9–11)** — real results, real customer, specific and relatable; soft CTA.
**Email 6 — Differentiation (day 12–14)** — why your approach differs; address the alternatives honestly; build preference.
**Email 7 — Objection handler (day 15–18)** — the top concern, answered; FAQ or myth-busting; reduce friction.
**Email 8 — Direct offer (day 19–21)** — clear pitch, specific CTA, urgency only if real.

## Re-engagement sequence

**Trigger:** 30–60 days of inactivity (no open, no click, no product use). **Length:** 3–4 emails over 2 weeks.
**Goal:** win back or clean the list — both outcomes are wins. **Exit:** any engagement, or suppression after the final email.

**Email 1 — Check-in (at trigger)**
- Subject shape: "Is everything okay, [Name]?"
- Genuine concern, not a pitch; ask what happened
- Offer one easy way back in

**Email 2 — Value reminder (2–3 days later)**
- Subject shape: "Remember when you [achieved X]?"
- Recall past value; summarize what's new since they drifted
- One quick CTA

**Email 3 — Incentive (5–7 days later)**
- Subject shape: "We miss you — here's something special"
- Time-boxed offer, if an offer is appropriate at all
- Clear CTA

**Email 4 — Last chance (10–14 days later)**
- Subject shape: "Should we stop emailing you?"
- Honest and direct; one click to stay, one click to go
- No response means suppress. Keeping dead addresses costs deliverability for everyone still reading.

## Onboarding sequence (product users)

**Trigger:** account created / trial started. **Length:** 5–7 emails over 14 days.
**Goal:** reach the aha moment, then expand. **Exit:** activated + converted, or trial expired (hand off to the expired-trial win-back).

Coordinate with [[user-onboarding]]: the in-app flow owns the activation path; email nudges the people who fell out of it. Emails 2 and 5 below are branch-only — send them to non-completers, not to everyone.

**Email 1 — Welcome + first step (immediate)** — confirm signup, name one critical action, link straight to it.
**Email 2 — Getting-started help (day 1, branch: step 1 incomplete)** — one tip or short video, plus a support option.
**Email 3 — Feature highlight (day 2–3)** — the key capability they should know, tied to a specific use case, in-app link.
**Email 4 — Success story (day 4–5)** — a customer whose journey resembles theirs; motivational, short.
**Email 5 — Check-in (day 7, branch: low activity)** — how's it going, ask for feedback, offer help.
**Email 6 — Advanced tip (day 10–12)** — a power feature for engaged users; level-up content.
**Email 7 — Upgrade / expand (day 14+)** — trial users get the conversion push, free users the upgrade prompt, paid users the expansion opportunity.
