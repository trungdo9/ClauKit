# Post-Launch Product Marketing

The launch isn't over when the announcement goes live. Adoption and retention work starts there.

## Immediate actions

**Educate new users.** Set up an automated onboarding email sequence introducing key features and use cases.

**Reinforce the launch.** Include the announcement in the next weekly/biweekly/monthly roundup email to catch everyone who missed the moment.

**Differentiate against competitors.** Publish comparison pages explaining why you're the obvious choice for the buyer who is now aware of you.

**Update web pages.** Add dedicated sections about the new product or feature across the site — the launch changed what the site should say.

**Offer a hands-on preview.** Build a no-code interactive demo (e.g. Navattic or similar) so visitors can explore before signing up.

## Keep the momentum

It is far easier to build on existing momentum than to restart from zero. Every touchpoint after launch day reinforces the launch rather than competing with it.

## Ongoing launch strategy

Don't rely on a single event. Regular updates and feature rollouts sustain engagement between big moments.

### Prioritize what to announce

**Major updates** (new features, product overhauls)
- Full campaign across multiple channels: blog post, email campaign, in-app messages, social.
- Maximize exposure.

**Medium updates** (new integrations, UI enhancements)
- Targeted announcement: email to relevant segments, in-app banner.
- No full fanfare needed.

**Minor updates** (bug fixes, small tweaks)
- Changelog and release notes.
- Signals that the product is improving without dominating the marketing.

### Announcement tactics

- **Space out releases.** Stagger announcements instead of shipping everything at once — each ship is a chance to be seen.
- **Reuse what worked.** If a previous announcement resonated, apply the same structure and channel mix to the next one.
- **Keep engaging.** Email, social, and in-app messaging continue to highlight improvements between launches.
- **Signal active development.** Even small changelog entries remind customers the product is evolving; that builds retention and word of mouth because customers trust you'll still be here.
