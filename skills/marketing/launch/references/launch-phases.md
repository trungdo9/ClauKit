# The Five-Phase Launch Ramp

Launching isn't a one-day event. Each phase de-risks the next and builds momentum.

## Phase 1 — Internal launch

Gather initial feedback and iron out major issues before going public.

- Recruit early users one-on-one to test for free.
- Collect feedback on usability gaps and missing features.
- Ensure the prototype is functional enough to demo — it does not need to be production-ready.

**Goal:** validate core functionality with friendly users.

## Phase 2 — Alpha launch

Put the product in front of external users in a controlled way.

- Create a landing page with an early-access signup form.
- Announce that the product exists.
- Invite users individually to start testing.
- The MVP should be working in production, even if still evolving.

**Goal:** first external validation, waitlist starts building.

## Phase 3 — Beta launch

Scale up early access while generating external buzz.

- Work through the early-access list (some free, some paid).
- Start marketing with teasers about the problem you solve.
- Recruit friends, investors, and influencers to test and share.

**Consider adding:**
- A coming-soon landing page or waitlist.
- A "Beta" sticker in the dashboard navigation.
- Email invites to the early-access list.
- An early-access toggle in settings for experimental features.

**Goal:** buzz, plus product refinement from broader feedback.

## Phase 4 — Early access launch

Shift from small-scale testing to controlled expansion.

- Leak product details: screenshots, feature GIFs, demos.
- Gather quantitative usage data alongside qualitative feedback.
- Run user research with engaged users (incentivize with credits).
- Optionally run a product/market-fit survey to refine messaging.

**Expansion options:**
- **A — throttled:** invite in batches, 5–10% at a time. Controlled load, slower momentum.
- **B — all at once:** invite the whole list under an "early access" framing. Bigger moment, less control.

**Goal:** validate at scale and prepare for full launch.

## Phase 5 — Full launch

Open the floodgates.

- Open self-serve signups.
- Start charging, if not already.
- Announce general availability across all channels.

**Launch touchpoints:**
- Customer emails
- In-app popups and product tours
- Website banner linking to launch assets
- "New" sticker in dashboard navigation
- Blog post announcement
- Social posts across platforms
- Platform listings — Product Hunt, BetaList, Hacker News, and similar

**Goal:** maximum visibility and conversion to paying users.

## Mapping phases to a calendar

The phases compress or stretch to fit the window; they don't disappear. For a short runway (e.g. six weeks), the usual shape is: internal in week 1, alpha in weeks 1–2 while the landing page collects the waitlist, beta in weeks 3–4 alongside borrowed-channel outreach, early access in week 5, full launch in week 6. Audience-building runs across every phase — a launch day aimed at a list that was never built is a press release into an empty room.
