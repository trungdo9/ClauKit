# Platform playbook

Hashtags do a different job on every platform, and the counts differ. Never paste one block everywhere.
Match the approach to where the audience actually is.

> Counts, caps, and behaviors below are 2026 norms. **Re-verify quarterly** — platforms change these
> without much notice, and a stale cap gets posts trimmed or blocked.

## Instagram

- **Hard cap: 5 hashtags** (enforced, rolled out December 2025). More are blocked or trimmed.
- Use **3–5 precise, relevant** tags — niche, topic, community, location, or branded. Not generic.
- **Mid-tier tags (~10K–500K posts)** beat mega-tags. On a mega-tag you are buried in seconds.
- **Placement: in the caption**, indexed immediately for search. Some large accounts put tags in the first
  comment for a clean caption; for most brands the caption is better for SEO.
- **Hashtag-following was removed** (December 2024) — tags no longer feed subscribers. They are search and
  category signals only.
- **Reels:** lean on trending audio plus keywords. Hashtags just categorize; audio often does more for reach.
- **The real lever:** a keyword-rich caption plus on-screen text. Instagram indexes both.

## TikTok

- **3–5 hashtags**, and **treat them as keywords**: `#BestBudgetLaptop`, not `#Tech`. One broad category tag
  plus niche tags that match real searches.
- **TikTok is a search engine.** The ranking move is the **triple mention** — the primary keyword spoken,
  on-screen, and in the caption, front-loaded.
- **`#fyp` and `#viral` are useless.** They do not affect For You Page placement.
- **Avoid the 30-tag spray** — TikTok deprioritizes it.
- Optional: a **pinned keyword-rich comment** acts like a second caption.

## LinkedIn

- **3–5 specific** hashtags, treated as a lightweight topic signal rather than a reach driver. LinkedIn has
  deprioritized hashtag distribution — its own product lead has called them "nice to have, not need to
  have" — and its algorithm reads semantic meaning from the post body directly.
- The lever is **keywords in the post body**. If you tag, specific beats broad: `#SaaSLeadership` over
  `#Leadership`. Never a broad-tag pile.

## X / Twitter

- **0–2, often zero.** X has deprioritized hashtags and has called them clutter in its own ad guidance.
  More than one or two can *reduce* engagement.
- Use a single tag only to join a specific conversation or live event.

## Threads

- **One topic tag per post.** Keep it relevant; do not force it.

## Facebook

- **Minimal — 1–2** relevant tags at most. Hashtags do little for reach here. The discovery lever on
  Facebook is the caption and transcript, which the algorithm reads directly → [[facebook-strategy]].

## Pinterest

- **Keywords in the title and description are the lever** — Pinterest is a visual search engine. Hashtags
  are minor and effectively deprecated. Do not rely on them.

## YouTube

- **Keywords in the title and description** drive search. A few relevant tags help; the first ~3 hashtags
  can display above the title. Do not stuff.

## Quick reference

| Platform | Hashtags | The real lever |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 3–5 (hard cap 5) | keyword caption + on-screen text; trending audio on Reels |
| TikTok | 3–5, as keywords | triple-mention keyword (spoken + on-screen + caption) |
| LinkedIn | 3–5 specific (light signal) | keywords in the body; tags do not drive reach |
| X / Twitter | 0–2 | the post itself; tag only to join a moment |
| Threads | 1 topic tag | the post plus its topic tag |
| Facebook | 1–2 | the post itself; the algorithm reads captions and transcripts |
| Pinterest | minor | keywords in title and description |
| YouTube | a few | keywords in title and description |
