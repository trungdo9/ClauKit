# Examples — discoverability sets in practice

Worked examples showing the **keyword and SEO layer first**, then a tight tag set per platform, plus a
branded-tag design — and a contrast with the old 30-tag block.

Running topic: a SaaS founder posting about deleting their most-requested feature. Placeholder brand:
**Northpad**. Adapt tags to the real account and niche, and always vet each one on-platform first.

---

## Example 1 — Instagram Reel (hard cap 5)

**Keyword / SEO layer — the real lever:**

- **Caption, front-loaded:** "Why we deleted our most-requested SaaS feature — and what we built instead."
  Primary keyword: *SaaS feature* / *product decisions*.
- **On-screen text:** "We deleted our #1 requested feature."
- **Spoken:** say "most-requested feature" and "product decisions" aloud.
- **Audio:** a relevant trending sound. On Reels, audio does more for reach than tags.

**Hashtags — 3–5, niche or mid-tier, in the caption:**

`#BuildInPublic #SaaSProduct #ProductManagement #StartupLessons #IndieHackers`

Optional branded tag in place of one: `#NorthpadBuildLog`.

→ Five tags, all relevant, all mid-tier, no generic mega-tags. Respects the cap.

---

## Example 2 — TikTok (tags as keywords)

**Triple-mention keyword:** "delete a feature" / "SaaS product decisions" — **spoken, on-screen, and
front-loaded in the caption.**

**Caption:** "How we decide what to build for our SaaS (we deleted our most-requested feature)."

**Hashtags — 3–5, as keywords, one broad plus niche:**

`#SaaS #BuildInPublic #StartupTok #ProductDecisions #FoundersOfTikTok`

→ `#StartupTok` and `#FoundersOfTikTok` signal the right side of TikTok. No `#fyp`. The keywords, not the
tags, do the ranking.

---

## Example 3 — LinkedIn (3–5 specific)

Keywords live in the post body naturally; LinkedIn indexes them.

**Hashtags:** `#SaaS #ProductManagement #BuildInPublic` — specific, not `#Leadership` or `#Business`.

---

## Example 4 — X / Twitter

**Zero or one tag.** The post carries itself. Add a single tag only to join a live conversation. Usually
none.

---

## Example 5 — Facebook

**One or two at most**, and only if genuinely relevant. The discovery lever here is the caption and the
video transcript, which the algorithm reads directly → [[facebook-strategy]].

---

## Example 6 — a branded and a campaign hashtag

**Branded (ongoing):** `#NorthpadBuildLog` — short, unique, on-brand. Goes in the bio and on every build-log
post; followers use it to share their own build updates.

**Campaign (time-bound):** `#NorthpadLaunchWeek` — for a launch, promoted in the CTA ("post your setup with
#NorthpadLaunchWeek"), tracked through native search.

Both checked first to confirm they are unused and typo-safe.

---

## Contrast — the old block (do not do this)

`#love #viral #fyp #explore #instagood #follow4follow #marketing #business #entrepreneur #success
#motivation #socialmedia #digitalmarketing #growth #contentcreator …(×30)`

→ Generic mega-tags that bury the post. `#fyp` does nothing. Over the Instagram cap. Identical on every
post. Zero relevance signal. When a user asks for this, redirect to a tight relevant set plus the keyword
layer — then still hand them a set, so the answer is useful and not just a lecture.

---

## What the good examples share

- **Keywords and SEO done first** — caption front, on-screen, spoken — because that is what drives discovery.
- A **tight, relevant, mid-tier** tag set, **per platform**, within caps.
- **No generic mega-tags**, no `#fyp`, no 30-tag block.
- A **branded tag** where community or UGC applies.
- Honest framing: tags categorize and aid search; content and engagement drive reach.
